Weather Radar
Weather Radar page

Adds a full-screen page showing the latest National Weather Service radar picture. Choose a single radar site, a regional view, or the whole lower 48; the picture refreshes at the update interval you choose.

Crop zooms the map to fill the screen and hides the National Weather Service banner and colour scale; leave it off to see the whole published picture with black bars above and below. Coverage is the United States and its territories only.

Enable Weather Radar page
Adds the Weather Radar page and starts fetching the selected area.
Show Overlay
Area name and update time overlay on the display
Dark mode
Darkens the map background and outlines so the page is not blinding in a dark room. Turn it off to see the picture exactly as the National Weather Service publishes it, on a white background. The rain and storm colours look the same either way. The Red Night theme always uses the dark map, whatever this is set to.
Crop
Zoom the map to fill the screen and hide the National Weather Service banner and colour scale. The sides are trimmed too, more so on wide views like the national map; turn it off to see the whole picture with bars above and below.
How often the radar picture refreshes. The National Weather Service publishes a new picture about every 2 minutes, so checking more often than that gains nothing.
This also changes the look of the animation over time. When the page first loads it fills the animation with the ten most recent pictures, which are about 2 minutes apart, so it starts as a smooth 20 minute replay. From then on it adds one new picture each time it refreshes, so the older ones gradually age out and the replay stretches. At 15 minutes, after a couple of hours the animation covers about 2.5 hours in 15 minute jumps: a longer story of the weather, but a choppier one. A shorter interval keeps the animation smooth and close to real time, at the cost of more network traffic.
How much recent weather the page plays back. Each frame is one radar picture taken about 2 minutes apart, so 10 frames is roughly the last 20 minutes; 1 frame is a still picture that never animates. Each extra frame is another picture the display keeps in memory, so pick a shorter animation if the device is short on room.
Automatic uses the radar nearest the latitude and longitude set on the System tab, and falls back to the national view when no site is close enough.
